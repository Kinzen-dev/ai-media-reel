Preface

Every screen you read from was set by someone who never saw it at your size, in your light, at your distance, on a late train or at three in the morning.

The blue hairline across this page is a reading line. It moves one to one with the scroll, counts the line it crosses in the left margin, and reports that line’s measure on the gauge above. Chapter seven is about when that helps, and when it gets in the way.

Measure: a line’s length in characters. The main text holds 66; the gauge marks the comfortable 45 to 75.

We teach typography to people who build software. The questions they bring are rarely about typefaces. They want to know why a settings page feels tiring by the fourth row, why the same contract reads better on paper, and why dark mode makes their text look heavier than it is.

Measure is our answer, written down. It isn’t a style guide, and it won’t tell you the right line height. It shows you what each decision does, lets you push it past the point where it breaks, and leaves the choosing to you.

The plates

Measure runs in the browser. Fourteen chapters, each built around a plate you can scrub, drag and break, with the CSS beside it and a note on what it costs in practice. It is written for the designers who set text and the engineers who ship it, and it assumes you know what a pixel is but not what a pica is.

Plate seven is the only one that moves while you read it. The chapter asks whether anything should.

Text-box trimming, pretty wrapping and scroll-driven animation have all reached browsers in the last three years. The plates use them where they help, show the fallback beside them, and say plainly when they don’t help at all.

Contents

Principles 01 to 07 are short essays on one decision each, and are best read in order. Prototypes 08 to 14 are complete components with their source, and can be read in any order once you know the principles they lean on.

Questions

Do I need to know CSS?
No. Every plate works by dragging and scrubbing. The CSS sits beside it for the engineers, and you can read the whole manual without opening it.
Is it a course with deadlines?
There are no lessons to complete and nothing to hand in. Read the principles in order, then reach for the prototypes when a project needs them.
Which browsers does it support?
Any current browser. Where a plate depends on a newer feature, such as text-box trimming, it names the feature, shows the fallback and tells you what you lose.
Each plate lists the browsers it was last tested in, with the date of the test.
Can I use the prototypes in client work?
Yes. The source is MIT licensed. Credit is welcome and never required.
How long does the edition last?
Edition two is updated until the end of 2027. Everything published before then stays yours.
Who writes it?
Letterkaai, a type school of nine people in Antwerp. We have taught reading on screens since 2016, mostly to people who build software.
Letterkaai is Flemish for letter quay. The school is one street back from the Scheldt.
