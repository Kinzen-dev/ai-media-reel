      About the series

      The main staircase of the Carrow Library was built in 1911 to carry readers from the lending hall up to the reading room: forty-four stone treads turning once round a single newel. Every other Thursday this autumn and winter it carries something else. Eighty people sit on the steps, a speaker stands at the foot, and for an hour the busiest stair in the building becomes its quietest room.

      The lectures are free and always have been. Each is fifty minutes on one subject by someone who has spent years on it, then questions from the steps. Nothing is filmed. Everything is transcribed, and the transcripts are shelved in the reading room at the top of the stair, catalogued like any other book.

      How to attend

      Seats
      Eighty, on the treads, first come. No booking and no tickets. Cushions are handed out at the door; the stone is cold by November.
      Doors
      18:30 at the Wherry Street entrance. The lecture begins at 19:00 and the stair is clear by 20:15.
      Access
      Twelve chairs at the foot of the stair are kept for anyone who would rather not sit on a step. The reading room landing has step-free space for four wheelchairs, reached by the lift behind the issue desk. A hearing loop covers the whole stairwell.
      Transcripts
      Every lecture is transcribed live onto a screen at the foot of the stair. The full text is on the reading room shelves within a week, and in large print on request.
      Children
      Welcome with an adult. The lowest treads are theirs.

      Past seasons

      Seasons one to four were held here, on this stair. While it was taken apart and relaid between 2024 and 2026, the lectures went to whatever stair the building could spare.

        Colophon

        The Stair Lectures are organised by the Friends of the Carrow Library with the library’s adult learning team, and paid for by the Friends’ annual book sale. Programme by Wen Aldous; doors, cushions and the hearing loop by Patrick Oyelaran. Suggest a speaker, or offer to be one, at the issue desk or by letter to the Friends, Carrow Library, 14 Wherry Street.
